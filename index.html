Bare-Dirt Step-by-Step Construction Guide

Step 1 of 8

Step 1: Bare Dirt Surface Prep & Raking

Est. Time: ~45 Mins (Fast!)

Since your yard is already 100% bare dirt, NO grass removal or sod digging is required! You save over 8 hours of backbreaking shoveling. Simply clear surface rocks and rake soil roughly level.

Instructions:

  1. Mark out your 150-foot path with marking spray paint and string line (36 inches wide).
  2. Clear away loose surface rocks, sticks, or root debris from the bare dirt surface.
  3. Use a standard bow rake to level high dirt spots into low spots.
  4. Lightly tamp loose dirt spots with your boots or an 8x8 hand tamper. No deep trenching needed!
Bare Dirt Bonus: You start on an ideal flat foundation. The sand cushion will easily absorb minor soil micro-bumps!
Step 1 Diagram: Flat Bare Dirt Prep
2

Step 2: Geotextile Fabric Direct Laydown

Est. Time: ~45 Mins

Unrolling heavy 4ft commercial non-woven fabric directly over bare dirt creates an indestructible subgrade membrane that prevents dirt and sand from mixing.

Instructions:

  1. Unroll your 4 ft wide geotextile fabric roll down the 150-foot path directly over bare soil.
  2. Allow fabric to extend 6 inches wider than the path on both sides.
  3. Overlap roll joints by 8 to 12 inches.
  4. Anchor into raw soil using steel fabric pins or landscape staples every 4 feet.
High-Poodle Impact Protection: Geotextile fabric distributes standard poodle sprinting weight over bare soil, preventing deep rutting!
Step 2 Diagram: Fabric Over Bare Soil
3

Step 3: Spike-Anchored Plastic L-Edging Installation

Est. Time: ~1.5 Hours

Heavy-duty plastic L-edging strips pinned through fabric into firm dirt using 10-inch spiral steel spikes create outer retention walls to cradle red border bricks.

Instructions:

  1. Position L-edging strips along outer edges (36" apart) with the flat base lip facing inward.
  2. Drive 10" spiral steel spikes every 12 inches through edging holes and fabric deep into undisturbed subsoil.
  3. Use 3lb sledge hammer. Space spikes every 6 inches on tight curves.
Step 3 Diagram: Spiked Plastic Edging
4

Step 4: Red Clay Brick Border Placement

Est. Time: ~1.5 Hours

Laying classical red clay border bricks flat against the inside wall of the plastic L-edging frames your pathway visually and prevents side stone spillover.

Instructions:

  1. Lay red border bricks end-to-end on top of the geotextile fabric along both outer edges.
  2. Press bricks snugly against the vertical plastic wall of the spiked L-edging.
  3. Ensure brick tops sit uniform and level along the path length.
Step 4 Diagram: Red Brick Border
5

Step 5: Levelling Sand Bed Screeding

Est. Time: ~1 Hour

Pouring a 1.5-inch bed of coarse sand over the fabric between the red brick borders creates a cushion bed for pavers.

Instructions:

  1. Pour coarse concrete sand over geotextile fabric between red border bricks.
  2. Use a notched 2x4 board resting on red border bricks to drag and screed sand to a uniform **1.5-inch depth**.
  3. Lightly mist with water to settle loose dust.
Step 5 Diagram: Screeded Sand Layer
6

Step 6: Flush Center Paver Placement

Est. Time: ~1.5 Hours

Setting square 12" x 12" stepping pavers centered down the path creates flat targets tailored for human feet and standard poodle strides.

Instructions:

  1. Set 12" x 12" pavers centered down path, spaced **24 inches apart on-center**.
  2. Tap pavers down into sand bed with rubber mallet until top surface sits **completely flush** with red border bricks.
  3. Check with spirit level to eliminate tripping hazards or dewclaw snags.
Standard Poodle Stride Safety: Flush pavers prevent sprinting dogs from catching dewclaws during high-speed backyard runs!
Step 6 Diagram: Flush Center Pavers
7

Step 7: 3/8" Angular Stone / DG Infill Spreading

Est. Time: ~1 Hour

Filling gaps around pavers with 3/8" angular crushed stone or decomposed granite (DG) creates a tight, self-locking aggregate matrix that won't scatter under high-speed running.

Instructions:

  1. Pour 3/8" angular crushed stone into open gaps around center pavers and red border bricks.
  2. Spread aggregate evenly with a stiff broom.
  3. Fill stone to sit **1/8 inch below** top paver surfaces.
No Pea Gravel Warning: Never use smooth round pea gravel! Jagged 3/8" angular aggregate interlocks tight under dog paws.
Step 7 Diagram: Angular Stone Matrix
8

Step 8: Water Settling & Simple Hand Tamping

Est. Time: ~45 Mins

Skip renting a 200lb plate compactor! Saturating the path with hose water lubricates rock dust fines, causing aggregate to settle naturally into a stone crust.

Instructions:

  1. Attach spray nozzle to garden hose set to fine shower/mist mode.
  2. Soak path until water pools slightly on stone infill.
  3. Let water soak for 15 minutes to bind rock dust.
  4. Walk over path with 8" x 8" manual hand tamper to firmly pack infill around pavers.
Project Complete! Your low-labor, dog-proof backyard pathway is ready for high-speed standard poodle test runs!

Bare Dirt & Poodle Safety FAQ

Will weeds grow through on bare dirt?

Commercial non-woven geotextile fabric creates a permanent physical barrier preventing dirt-borne weeds from pushing up.

Do 10" steel spikes hold in raw soil?

Yes! 10-inch spiral steel spikes driven every 12 inches bind into subsoil, easily absorbing high-speed 45lb poodle cornering thrust.

How to maintain angular stone?

Keep a spare 50lb bag of 3/8" angular aggregate in your garage. If high-speed cornering creates minor low spots, sprinkle and mist once a year.
